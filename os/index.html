OS  ·  Monad OS

An operating system for one person.

I hold down a law-enforcement job, a trading operation, a coaching practice, a book, and a family. No one keeps that in their head. So I built a system that keeps it for me, with AI doing the clerical work and me making the decisions.

The parts

Three pieces, all plain text.

  • A morning brief. Each morning an AI agent reads my records (calendar, open tasks, the state of each project, anything that broke overnight) and writes one short page: what needs me today, and what's broken. It reads only from records. If something isn't written down, the brief doesn't pretend to know it.
  • A decision ledger. Every real decision gets an entry: the date, what was decided, and why, in my own words. When I'm tempted to reopen a settled question at 11 p.m., the ledger tells me what I already knew when I was thinking clearly.
  • A knowledge base. Everything I read and learn, filed by area and linked together, so a book from last year can answer a question this week.

It all lives in ordinary text files I own, synced and backed up. The AI is a tool inside it, not the place it's stored.

The rules that keep it honest

Learned the expensive way.

  • Nothing is "done" without the evidence written next to it. A task gets checked off with the receipt: the confirmation number, the file, the screenshot. A checkmark with nothing behind it is how things quietly fall through.
  • Nothing fails silently. In September one stretch of my own automation failed eight times in eight days without telling anyone. Now every automated job reports its own failures, and checking the outcome against reality is part of the job, not an extra.
  • Subtract before adding. When the system starts to feel heavy, the first move is to remove a piece, not build a new one.
  • One list. Anything that needs me goes on one list. Not two, not a list per project.
Why it's on this site

It's the same method I sell.

The back-office work I do for local businesses is this system pointed at a company instead of a person: follow how information moves, write it down, let AI do the retyping, and watch every automation so it can't break quietly. I'd rather show you the one I live on, failures included, than describe one I don't.
